## Fleet RepairAI (C)

Native RepairAI assesses surviving hulls/components, queues bounded repair teams,
prioritizes heroes and mission-required units, and consumes real military spare
parts, structural alloy and repair kits from finite fleet inventories. A JSON
policy defines team count, duration/resource rates and the one-time campaign
supply allocation. This allocation is not repeated on load or deployment. Depots
remain in their systems and request resupply through existing routed freight;
stock is credited on unloading. Replacements use mass-based construction supplies
at an operational shipyard belonging to the pilot's empire. Construction remains
a native fleet work order rather than a player blueprint-manufacturing job.

Repairs preserve the physical HullID and installed grade, restore components and
readiness, and never refill ammunition. Destroyed originals remain in history;
rebuilt fighter/story slots receive a different HullID and Level III MILSPEC
components. Campaign-ending hero loss is not rebuilt. Active deployment, fresh
damage, missing materials, offline depots and unavailable allied shipyards suspend
or reassess work. Readiness unlocks before pristine condition when minimum hull,
power and propulsion thresholds are met. Allied Forces details show work status,
progress, remaining time and shortage reasons; enemy repair data stays hidden.

Work orders persist alongside their resource debit in the economy save. The
campaign snapshot carries applied-progress markers. A successful economy save
followed by a failed campaign save is reconciled idempotently on reload, including
legacy fleet-identity migration. Failure to save the debit rolls back both work
and inventory. The GameInstance adapter wakes on fleet/supply events and scheduled
milestones; it does not scan fleet repairs every frame and survives level changes.
This phase does not migrate the simulation onto a worker or validate a remote
server, and the repair-store/freight destination uses the system transfer endpoint.

Phase E - ship commerce and economy presentation

Sales retain a physical hull tombstone and return the same serial, installed
component IDs, condition and ammunition to dealer stock. A repurchase updates
that serial in place; it does not create another hangar row or heal the hull.
The active hull, mission loaners, rentals, loaded cargo and cargo reservations
cannot be sold. At least one serviceable owned fighter must remain; a rental
or mission loaner cannot bypass this rule. Dealers use existing finite budgets.
A dealer with insufficient credits or storage rejects the transaction.

New ship-commerce policy is authored in GameData/Ships/commerce.json: 70 percent
condition-adjusted buyback, and two percent of the design price per rental day.
These are SSW commerce policy choices, not recovered EVE balance values. Sale
prices show included existing taxes/fees, with net proceeds in the wallet.

Rentals are one, three or seven real days, with saved UTC deadlines, lessor,
return dock, hull identity and mandatory Basic insurance. Insurance uses the
existing premium/coverage formula on hull value only; no component-loss charge
is invented. Legal ownership remains with the NPC. Hull-loss liability is
max(0, hull value - insurance payout). Insurance pays the owner; an unpaid
remainder is retained as debt and prevents another rental until settled.
Expiry prevents a new launch or selection; an overdue craft is not despawned
mid-flight. Return requires the issuing dock, another active craft, and an
empty unreserved hold. The current damaged hull returns to dealer stock,
not a resurrected combat-squadron slot. No automatic fee refund or repair.

Hangar exposes confirmed sale/return actions, rental deadlines and liability
repayment. Ship Store exposes rental terms and mandatory-insurance quotes.
Skills group buttons have a common width and height; fixed icon, level and
price columns leave the skill name the remaining width. Ship Store previews
receive the shared drag/orbit, wheel zoom and double-click reset input.
